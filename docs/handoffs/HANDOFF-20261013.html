The plateau, the self-play fix, and D023

Runs D020 through D023, 11–13 October. Written so either of us can pick this up cold. It continues the 10 October handoff; read that for D005–D019, the policy-loss fixes and the memory fault. The source of truth is the mali_ba repo (branch main); open_spiel only holds symlinks.

Status on 13 October

  • D023 is the strongest model. It beat D021 0.453 (95% CI [0.409, 0.497]; an even match is 0.333) at the standard eval guidance of 0.55, the second-largest head-to-head gain in the project. Its value head is significantly better on held-out positions, and its search targets got sharper for the first time.
  • What changed: self-play move selection (section 3) and the buffer pool shares. Before D023, self-play was close to random play: ~465-move games, 70% timeouts. D023's self-play ended 85% of games with a natural win at a median of ~285 moves.
  • D019, D020 and D021 were level (three-way 0.323 / 0.327 / 0.350). The policy fixes in D021 cost nothing but gained nothing measurable on their own.
  • The guidance is still needed at play time. At guidance 0, D023 and D021 were even (0.347) and a quarter of the games timed out.
  • Seat 0 dominates, more so as play gets stronger. D023 self-play wins by seat: 55 / 34 / 11%. In the head-to-heads seat 2 won 19 of 495 decided games. This is now a game-design question (section 3).
  • Next run, D024: from D023, with training guidance lowered to a steady 0.10 (was 0.38) and the special rare-goods games turned off. Check the first 30 minutes (section 5).
  • No memory errors since the desktop rebooted on 11 October at 22:34 (D021 and D023 ran clean), but the MemTest steps in TODO.md are still not done.

1. The short version

  • D021 (the policy-loss fixes) showed the network already matches its search targets: the gap stayed at ~0.09 all run. The policy is flat because the targets are flat.
  • The targets were flat partly because self-play was nearly random. Every move, even the unrecorded 40-simulation ones, was sampled from near-flat visit counts at temperature 1.0 for 150 moves and 0.5 after. Games bore little resemblance to strong play, so the value head learned from long timeout games.
  • D023 changed how self-play picks moves (greedy fast moves, temperature 1.0 for only 30 moves, then 0.25) and rebalanced the buffer pools toward win positions. Self-play became decisive, the value head improved on held-out positions, target entropy fell, and D023 beat D021 0.453.
  • The heuristic is still needed: without it neither network can finish most games.
  • Turn order matters more as play improves; seat 2 is close to hopeless in strong play.

3. What we learned

The D019–D021 plateau

  • Value probe on a new held-out set (17,038 positions from the D021/D020/D019 three-way): D017 through D021 all scored 62–63% top-pick, all ~5 points above D013. No value-head progress after D017.
  • D021's POLICY FIT gap stayed at ~0.09 for the whole run, and target entropy rose slightly (1.74 → 1.79). The network fits its targets; the targets weren't getting sharper.
  • Legal moves per position (20 random games, 10,180 positions): median 5, 90th percentile 7. Only ~5% of positions have 20 or more, mostly large route decisions. A target entropy of ~1.75 nats is close to uniform over the typical ~6 moves.

  • The cause: move selection. Self-play sampled every move from visit counts at temperature 1.0 for 150 moves, then 0.5, including the 74% of moves searched with only 40 simulations and never recorded. With near-flat visits, many moves were close to a weighted random choice. ab_eval plays the most-visited move.
  • D023's self-play: fast moves greedy; full-search moves at temperature 1.0 for 30 moves, then 0.25. Dirichlet noise, random layouts and random token placement are unchanged, so games still vary.
  • Results over D023a: target entropy fell from 1.71 to 1.61, the first fall ever (gap steady at ~0.095). In-run value calibration improved steadily (top-pick 75 → 80%, error 0.355 → 0.328, average prediction on target). Natural wins got faster (median ~305 → 272 moves).
  • Game variety held up: Timbuktu's share of wins rose late in the run, rare goods held steady, and the spread of win lengths (p25–p75: 230–359) stayed as wide as at the start.
  • Value probe on the held-out set: D023 65.6% top-pick against D021 62.8% (+2.8 [0.7, 4.8]) and D019 62.2%; MSE 0.424 against 0.490 and 0.500; Timbuktu positions 68.2% against 60.3%. The first significant value-head gain since D017.

The pools had to follow the new data

  • Each pool is a first-in-first-out queue, and its fraction sets both its size and its share of every batch. Under the old shares (timeout + near-win 0.70) and D022's ~77% win rate, the timeout pools would have stayed mostly D021 data for ~16 hours, with new timeout positions trained ~5.8× and win positions ~1×.
  • D023's shares: near-win 0.20, rare goods 0.35, Timbuktu 0.30, timeout 0.15. That gives each pool ~2–3× reuse, with Timbuktu favoured. On restart the trainer keeps the newest entries of any pool that shrinks.
  • train_interval_seconds stayed at 12: reuse averaged 2.1–2.6×, and more frequent training would have raised the over-used pools further.

The heuristic is still needed at play time

  • D023 vs D021 at guidance 0: even (0.347), 123 of 501 games timed out, median 294 moves. At 0.55: D023 clearly stronger, 6 timeouts, median 214.
  • Without the heuristic's focus, a 300-simulation search over a flat policy spreads too thinly to finish games, and the networks' differences disappear. D023's gain shows through the value head working with the heuristic.
  • D024 tests lower training guidance (0.10). The risk: self-play drifts back toward long timeout games, which is what held D019–D021 back.

5. The plan

  1. D024: from D023, training guidance a steady 0.10, rare-goods actors off

    • Both heuristic_guidance_weight and heuristic_guidance_weight_final are set to 0.10, so the decay schedule doesn't move it.
    • Rare-goods actors off (rare_goods_actor_fraction 0.30 → 0): rare-goods wins are now 65% of D023's wins, so the special games (+0.05 guidance, +100 early simulations) aren't needed. D024 therefore changes two things, and a result can't be split between them.
    • First 30 minutes: natural wins should stay near D023's 85% and games near 300 moves. If wins drop sharply or timeouts climb past ~30%, go back to 0.38.
    • Over the run: target entropy (D023 ended at 1.61), the in-run value calibration, the win-type mix and the seat split.
    • Evaluate D024 vs D023 at 0.55 (comparable with everything so far), plus a guidance-0 game to see whether the networks need the heuristic less. Then value_probe.py.

  2. Sharpening the search targets, one change per run

    • Policy-target pruning (KataGo): strip visits caused by noise and forced exploration from recorded targets. Changes targets only, not play.
    • First-play urgency for large route decisions: OpenSpiel values unvisited children at 0 while the average value is about −0.3 to −0.5, so the search visits every legal move once. Negligible for 5–7 legal moves; it matters for route decisions with up to 300 candidates.
    • Larger fast budget (40 → 80), Gumbel-style targets, more simulations on recorded moves: see TODO.md for details and ordering.

  3. Seat balance

    Find out why seat 0 wins rare goods so much faster: per-seat goods over the game in the eval logs, and analyze_placements.py by seat. Starting goods and placement order are the likely levers. Any rules change resets comparability, so plan it between run series.

  4. Still open from earlier

    • The desktop's memory tests (TODO.md, first section).
    • Landmark planes (Timbuktu, desert cities, coast) if Timbuktu prediction stays weak; heuristic token placement as a run; human play (ENGINE_UI_SEPARATION.md).

8. Things that misled us (don't repeat)

  • Mixing up which eval was which. The D023 head-to-heads were reported back without their guidance setting, and Claude assumed the first was the guidance-0 game. It was the 0.55 one, and the conclusions came out backwards until the log headers were checked. Every ab_eval log starts with the agents and their guidance: read it from there, and put the setting in the log name.
  • In-run value checks look better in decisive games. D023's winner top-pick of 80–83% isn't comparable with D021's 71%: decisive games are easier to call. Compare runs only by head-to-head or the value probe.
  • "First-play urgency explains the flat targets" was wrong for typical positions. With 5–7 legal moves, visiting each once costs ~5 of 100–600 simulations. It matters only for large route decisions.
  • Head-to-heads at guidance 0 lose many games to timeouts (123 of 501), which leaves fewer decided games and wider intervals.
  • Pool fractions set batch shares as well as sizes. When the mix of game endings changes, the pools keep old data and over-train new positions in the scarce pools. Re-check pool reuse whenever the win rate moves a lot.
  • Seat balance tuned on weak play doesn't hold in strong play. Re-measure it whenever self-play gets more decisive.
