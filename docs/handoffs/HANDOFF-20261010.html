1. The short version

  1. More games per hour was the first big win. The playout cap (25% of moves get a full search, the rest 40 simulations) plus faster remote workers gave D007, the first head-to-head gain in the project: 0.499 against D005.
  2. Aux value targets helped. The value network now also learns how the game will end and how many moves remain. D010 beat D007 0.431.
  3. Turning culling off helped. Games that would have been cut short now play out and their data is kept. D013 beat D011 0.387 and became the reference model.
  4. Several ideas didn't help: a lower uct_c, training every 4 seconds, swapping the win-pool shares, keeping 1 position in 32 instead of 16, and lower guidance at play time.
  5. The rewards changed, and games got longer. A loss now costs −0.7 however the game ends (D015), and games run to 500 moves instead of 420 (D016). D015 alone was weaker. With 500-move games, D016 matched D013 and D017 may have passed it.
  6. The route-distance aux head plus a 12 s training interval made D019 the strongest model (0.412 against D013). Lower guidance alone (D018) didn't help.
  7. Next: read D020 (a plain continuation of D019), look into seat 2's weakness in strong play, and work on getting the policy to learn.

3. What we learned

In-run metrics don't predict head-to-head results

D011's value checks looked better than D010's, yet they were level head to head. D013's looked worse than D011's, yet it won. When a run changes what is played or kept, the in-run test changes with it. Only head-to-head play, or a fixed held-out set (value_probe.py), compares runs.

The value head is limited by data, not size

Three value networks trained from scratch on the same buffer, scored on held-out games: the current size reached 54.1% top-pick, a wider head 53.9%, and a network twice as deep and wide 52.0%. Bigger networks overfit sooner. D011's production head, trained on about 30,000 games, scored 57.6% on the same positions. More and more varied games remain the lever.

Training too often brings back memorization

D008 trained every 4 seconds; each position was trained on 5.5 times and the model got weaker. At 8 seconds D007 had reuse of 2.4×. With buffer_keep_every = 32, which halves the positions each game adds, reuse is now about 4.35×, close to D008's level (see the plan).

Culling discarded useful data

Culling threw away about 16% of the compute. With it off, would-be-culled games ended in a win only 5.2% of the time (17/326), so the cull rules are about 95% right that a game won't produce a win. But keeping those games' data made D013 stronger. When the score lead is large, the current leader is the final timeout leader at least 96% of the time: a lead of 125 points or more at moves 320–359, 75 or more at moves 360–399, 50 or more from move 400. A safer future option is to end such games early while keeping their data.

The reward change: losers no longer end games, but that habit came from weak search

  • Under the old rewards a loser got −0.1 when a win condition ended the game but −0.7 at a timeout. So in self-play, 58% of Timbuktu endings were triggered by a player who then lost.
  • After the change (D015) that fell to 19%, and the self-play win rate halved (23% → 12%). The lost wins were the gifted ones. Timbuktu wins the eventual winner triggered held steady at about 6.6% of games.
  • But at full search strength the bots almost never hand a win to someone else. ab_eval now records who ended each game: D013 did so in 7 of 154 decided games it ended, D014 in 1 of 114. The habit comes from self-play's 40-simulation moves.
  • So the change fixed little in strong play while halving win positions, and D015 was weaker. We kept it anyway, because it matches the real game.

Longer games add wins

Wins arrive at a steady rate of about 0.9% of games per 10 moves from move 340 up to the cap, with no pile-up just before it. At 500 moves, desktop self-play in D016 rose from 21% to 27% wins. The gain came from games won by move 420 (11% → 16–17%). Late wins in moves 421–500 stayed at about 10–11% of games. D017 continued from 26% to 30%, mostly through more rare-goods wins.

The policy is still flat

Policy probe (8 October): entropy 92.0% of uniform for D007, 93.2% for D012. The policy's effective batch is small: only full-search moves train it, about 36 of every 128 rows.

The Timbuktu ending is a checklist, and the network can't see the landmarks

A trade route needs no adjacency: it's any set of 3–8 of a player's trading centers. The engine ends the game when a declared route includes Timbuktu, a desert city (Agadez or Oudane), at least 2 other cities and a coastal hex. The observation has a single plane marking every city alike, and nothing for Timbuktu, the desert cities or the coast. So the network has to learn from absolute board position which city is which, and convolutions are bad at that. That fits the value head anticipating rare-goods wins (visible in the goods planes) better than Timbuktu wins. An earlier guess, that it struggled to follow route connections, was wrong.

Starting positions matter

analyze_placements.py on 18,973 games from D011–D013, as a seat-adjusted regression with game-clustered errors. Effects on win chance, in percentage points:

  • spread between a player's tokens: +4.3
  • distance to the nearest city: −3.2
  • distance from Timbuktu: +1.8
  • number of cultures covered: +1.4
  • sitting in seat P2: −3.2

A heuristic placement that uses these weights is built and off by default (placement_mode).

D019: the route head learns, and the model got stronger

  • Route head: by the end of D019 its loss was 0.28× the always-mean baseline, and its error fell from 1.12 to 0.82 build steps, still falling. Labelled rows grew from 6% to 60% of each batch as new games replaced the old buffer.
  • Value head on Timbuktu games: top-pick 67% (D017), 69% (D018), 71% (D019); within D019, 70 / 69 / 72% by thirds. That points the right way but is within noise, since D018 swung as much.
  • Training interval: 12 s cut reuse from 4.35× to 2.97×.
  • Self-play wins rose from 23.0% to 26.8% during D019 (+3.8 ± 2.9 points), mostly rare-goods wins.
  • Strength: D019 beat both its predecessors in the three-way and beat D013 two-way. The two changes were made together, so their effects can't be separated; both are cheap to keep.
  • Shutdown fixes confirmed: at the end of D019 the trainer and the laptop worker both exited on their own.

Seat 2 wins far less in strong play

In self-play the seats are balanced (about 33/34/33% of wins). At 300 simulations they aren't:

  • D019 vs D013 (two-way): seat 0 209, seat 1 140, seat 2 73 decided games (17% for seat 2).
  • D019 / D018 / D017 three-way: 212 / 228 / 39 (8% for seat 2), and every agent was weak from seat 2.
  • Earlier evals had seat 2 at about 23%. Games are getting shorter (three-way median 258 moves), which may finish them before seat 2's extra starting goods pay off. Not yet investigated.

Seat 0 wins most often at full strength

In self-play the seats are now balanced (about 33/34/33% of wins) thanks to the setup rules: starting common goods 0/1/2 by seat, and serpentine token placement. In head-to-heads at 300 simulations, seat 0 still takes about 45% of decided games and seat 2 about 23%.

4. What was built or fixed

Training

  • Playout cap, with large route decisions exempt. Remote workers now receive every server setting; previously most were silently dropped.
  • Actor-side trajectory thinning: laptop uploads went from 71 MB to about 4.5 MB per game, and results had been arriving 11–14 minutes late.
  • Aux value targets (win type, moves left). They're trained by the learner only and saved in a _value_aux file.
  • Inference server pins itself to fast cores (inference_cpus = auto): the V-cache CCD on the desktop, the P-cores on the laptop.
  • Remote watchdog: a worker restarts itself if the trainer receives none of its games for 20 minutes or stops answering. It came after a Wi-Fi blip silently lost 411 laptop games.
  • Setup rules for seat balance: starting_common_goods_per_seat, serpentine_token_placement.
  • Culling settings in the ini (stalled_thresh, declining_best_thresh; random_no_kill_thresh = 1.0 turns culling off).
  • Heuristic token placement, with a random control fraction (off).
  • Fixes:
    • Dispatch now continues until num_episodes games are received.
    • The value step was skipped on batches without aux rows.
    • Shutdown hangs on both machines: the trainer exits cleanly, and remote workers stop on the trainer's stop signal instead of respawning. Untested at the end of a full run.

4b. The route-distance aux target (10 October, merged as fb0a4a2)

  • The target: for every player, the build steps still needed for the Timbuktu ending, from 0 to 10. A hex with the player's trading center counts 0, one with a post counts 1, and anything else counts 2. The checklist is Timbuktu, the cheaper desert city, the cheapest two other cities, and a coastal hex, which a coastal city can provide. It ignores what the rules might block (goods for upgrades, other players' posts, declaring the route), so it's a training signal, not a rule.
  • route_features.py computes it from the state JSON, the city list and the ini's coastal_hexes, in about 0.3 ms per position. It's Python only, so no C++ rebuild.
  • Actors label every step before the move, as an optional fifth item. The trainer carries it as a third aux item. Older buffer entries, and games from workers running older code, are left out of the route loss only.
  • The value network gets a separate aux_route head, built last, so the value path, search and evaluation are unchanged. _value_aux files load whether or not they include the route head; checked with D017, whose value, win-type and moves-left outputs came out identical.
  • Settings: aux_route_target = true, aux_route_weight = 1.0 (needs aux_targets).
  • analyze_log has an "AUX ROUTE HEAD" section: the route loss against always predicting the batch average, and the error in build steps. WANT: the loss well below that baseline and falling.
  • Smoke test (24 games from D018, empty buffer, scratch paths): D018's head file loaded through the fallback, with only the route head starting fresh. Every position with aux targets got a route label. The route loss fell below the always-mean baseline within about 10 training steps, reaching about half of it (0.018 against 0.045) and an error of about 1.1 build steps. The run saved and exited cleanly, and the saved file reloads with all heads.

5. The plan

  1. Read D020

    • analyze_log: the AUX ROUTE HEAD section (it starts with a buffer that already has route labels), Timbuktu-win value accuracy across the run, and the win-rate trend.
    • Head-to-head against D019. If D020 wins, continue the line; if it's level, D019's improvement may have plateaued.
    • Policy probe on D019 and D020, to see whether the policy has started to sharpen (entropy below about 92%).

  2. Building on D019

    D019 took both prepared changes at once, the route-distance aux target and the 12 s interval, and won. Next options, one per run:

    • Guidance back to 0.30: lowering it to 0.20 didn't help by itself (D018). Worth testing on the D019 line, since 0.30 gives more win positions.
    • If the route head learns but Timbuktu prediction stays weak, add landmark planes: Timbuktu, the desert cities and the coast. Give the network's first layer zero weights for the new channels, so it starts exactly as before. Landmarks are constant, so they can be appended exactly to every stored position. Route-progress planes would need a fresh buffer. Both need C++ changes and a rebuild.
    • Optional: raise the Timbuktu pool share to 0.15, taken from near-win (0.35 → 0.30). Don't take it from rare goods: D012's swap lost.

  3. Get the policy learning

    • A larger effective policy batch: a bigger batch, or extra full-search rows per step. The policy currently sees about 36 rows per step.
    • Per-node Q normalization (MuZero-style) is still a candidate. Lowering uct_c didn't help, so treat it with care, and test it head to head.

  4. Heuristic token placement as a run

    Built and ready (placement_mode = heuristic, 20% random control). It changes how starting positions are distributed in the training data, so give it a run of its own. Evaluations still place tokens at random for both agents.

  5. Later

    • Seat balance in strong play: seat 0 has about a 45% share of decided games at 300 simulations.
    • End settled games early while keeping their data, using the lead thresholds in section 3.
    • Human play: follow the migration steps in ENGINE_UI_SEPARATION.md, starting with GameSession and the bots, headless and tested.
    • Rules document: update the rare-goods end condition to five regions. The question of whether the Timbuktu route ends the game too easily is parked in TODO.md.
    • Extra compute (a second machine or hosted workers) is parked until training progress justifies it; see TODO.md.

  6. Housekeeping and run checklist

    • Rsync mali_ba.ini and the Python files to the laptop before every run, and rebuild both machines after any C++ change. In D016 the laptop played its first 1,400 games at 420 moves.
    • Check that a new run's first log lines show the expected weights, buffer and settings on both machines.
    • Don't edit the ini while a run or head-to-head is active: respawned actors and evals re-read it.

8. Things that misled us (don't repeat)

  • Self-play win rate isn't strength, and it can't be compared across a reward change: D015's halved win rate was gifted wins disappearing. Judge by head-to-head play.
  • In-run value checks don't compare across runs when the run changes what is played or kept. Use value_probe.py or head-to-head play.
  • Don't read interim head-to-head results from a rotation-ordered run. ab_eval used to play all A-in-seat-0 games first; an interim 0.51 finished at 0.346. Rotations are interleaved now.
  • Self-play behaviour isn't strong-play behaviour. The 58% loser-triggered endings vanished at 300 simulations.
  • A remote worker can silently use stale settings if it isn't rsynced. Check its log lines at the start of every run.
  • Changing max_play_moves rescales the move-count plane and the aux moves-left target. A saved buffer must be rescaled to match. It's exact: multiply both by old ÷ new, building new arrays, because oversampled entries share one array.
  • Changing rewards invalidates stored value targets. D014's buffer was relabelled exactly: non-winners in win-condition games, −0.6.
  • ab_eval's default guidance is 0.35, not the training value. It's kept for comparability with earlier evals.
  • Editing code while a run is active changes the run. The trainer starts fresh actor processes about every 10 games, and they import the live (symlinked) train_mali_ba.py. Develop in a separate git worktree and merge only after the run ends.
  • Launch the trainer through the open_spiel tree (/media/robp/UD/Projects/open_spiel/open_spiel/python/games/mali_ba), with the build's python directory first on PYTHONPATH. It finds the mali_ba package relative to its own path, and another pyspiel on the path breaks it.
  • Python's configparser crashes on inline ini comments in the sections it reads. Comments go on their own line.
