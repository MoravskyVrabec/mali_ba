# **Mali-ba: Rules Summary 0.8**

### **Background**

* Western Africa–a land the size of the United States and home to many different languages and cultures.  At its center is the city of Timbuktu on the mighty Niger River.  You are a small-time trader with a dream to set up your own trade network and collect rare & beautiful items from the far corners of the continent.  Or, perhaps, to control a trade route that connects the desert to the north with the seacoast to the south via Timbuktu. 

### 

### **Components**

* Hexagonal map of West Africa with cities and cultural regions   
* Meeples in various colors representing traders   
* Player tokens for each player  
* Trading post tokens for each player   
* Trading center tokens for each player   
* Common good and rare good resource cards 

### **Setup**

* Place meeples on the map, three chosen at random in each hex, including hexes with cities in them   
* Each player starts with a certain number of trading posts and trading centers  
* Each player starts with three player tokens  
* Each player, starting with the first player, places one of their player tokens in a hex on the board, followed by the next player, in a serpentine fashion, until each player has placed their three tokens

### **Core Mechanics**

#### **Actions**

* There are multiple actions a player can take, each of which takes the player’s turn.  
* There may also be a free action, which can be taken after the regular action of the player’s turn

#### **Action Types – A. Actions that consume the player’s turn when taken**

1. **Mancala Movement**:   
   * Choose a hex that contains one of your player tokens  
   * If there are no meeples in that hex, you may move your player token to an adjacent hex  
   * If there are meeples in the chosen hex, pick up your player token and all meeples in that hex   
   * Distribute one meeple (not your player token) per hex moving in any direction (do not leave a meeple in the original hex)  
   * After the meeples have all been placed on a hex, move to one more hex and place your player token  
   * You can only move between adjacent hexes and cannot enter a hex twice in one turn   
   * You may place a trading post in the hex where your player token finishes, as long as:  
     * there is one meeple in the hex, or  
     * you have one common good to turn in  
   * Remove from the game one meeple from the hex where you finish if you place a trading post.  If there is no meeple in the hex, turn in one common good of your choice.

2. **Take income**: When a player takes the “Take income” action, they will:   
   * Receive one rare good for each city where you have a trading center   
   * Receive two common goods or one rare good for each of your trading center(s) that is directly connected to a city/cities in a trade route. Examples:  
     * Red has a trading center that is connected to Oyo via a trade route, but no other cities. In this case, Red can choose to take two "Ivory" or one "Ivory bracelet"  
     * Red has a trading center that is connected to Segou and Sikasso via a trade route. In this case, Red can take a) one "Chiwara", b) one “Kora”, c) two “Millet”, d) two “Brass jewelry”, or e) one “Millet” and one “Brass jewelry”  
   * Receive two common goods for each trading center that is not connected to a city via a trade route (the goods correspond to the closest city or, if multiple cities are the same distance, the player chooses any combination of two common goods from the tied nearest cities) Example:  
     * Green has a trading center that is not connected to any cities by a trading route. Green is two hexes from both Segou and Sikasso. In this case, Green can take a) two “Millet”, b) two “Brass jewelry”, or c) one “Millet” and one “Brass jewelry”  
   * Receive one common good for each of your trading posts (the good corresponds to the closest city or, if multiple cities are the same distance, the player may choose)  
   * **Note**: goods correspond to the cities they come from (as per the cultural groups list)

3. **Upgrade Trading Post**:   
   * Turn in three common goods or one rare good to upgrade a trading post to a trading center  
   * A player may upgrade a trading post that is in a city if they wish

4. **Pass turn**:   
   * A player may pass the turn to the next player as an action if they wish. Free actions are still allowed during a turn when a player passes.

#### **Action Types – Declaring a Trade Route**

1. **Declare a Trade Route**: After upgrading a trading post to a trading center, you may declare connections between your trading centers and/or cities, forming a trade route, as long as the rules about trading posts & centers and trade routes are followed. 

#### **Trading Posts & Centers**

* Any number of players may have trading posts in a hex  
* At any time a player may only have one trading post or one trading center (not both) in a hex During a game with “n” players, only (n-1) trading centers can exist on a single non-city hex   
* Cities are exceptions: any number of players may have trading centers in a city

#### **Trade Routes**

* A trade route is created when a player takes the action “Declare a Trade Route”  
* Trade routes consist of connected trading centers  
* A trade route must connect a minimum of three trading centers  
* Any two of a player’s trade routes can contain a maximum of two of the same trading centers.   
* When declaring the trade route, the player states which hexes are connected with which  
* A given trade route may enter and exit a hex only once, but multiple trade routes may enter and exit the same hex

### **Regional Divisions**

The board is divided into geographical regions:

* Horizontal: Desert (north), Sahel (middle), Coastal (south)   
* Vertical: West, Central  
* Thus, the six geographical regions are: 1\. Desert West, 2\. Desert Center, 3\. Sahel West, 4\. Sahel Center, 5\. Coastal West, 6\. Coastal Center

### **Game End**

* The end of game is triggered when one of the following sets of conditions is met:  
* 1\. There are no legal moves available at the beginning of a player’s turn, or   
* 2\. A player has at least two trade routes, and either:   
  * a. That player has five different rare goods at the end of their turn, or  
  * b. A player’s trade route connects a desert city (Oudane or Agadez) with Timbuktu and a coastal hex via at least two other cities (a coastal city satisfies both the coastal hex and other city requirements in this case)

### **Scoring**

* Points for trading posts and trading centers on the board: 4 and 8 points respectively  
* Points for length of connected trade routes:  
  * Longest: 11 pts, second: 7 pts, third: 4 pts. Ties share the points, rounding down.  
* Points for collecting sets of unique resources:

| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 1 | 3 | 6 | 11 | 19 | 30 | 45 | 60 | 75 | 90 | 110 | \+20pts |

  * Example: Modibo has the following resources at the end of the game:  
    Camel (3), Cattle (3), Pepper (2), Kora (2), Chiwara (1), and Gold Crown (1).  
  * Looking at these, we see that Modibo has three sets of unique resources: Set 1 is Camel, Cattle, Pepper , Kora, Chiwara, and Gold Crown. This is six unique resources and so is worth 30 points. Set 2 is  Camel, Cattle, Pepper , and Kora. This is four unique resources, worth 11 points. Set 3 is Camel and Cattle, two unique resources worth 3 points.  
* Points for how many geographical areas each trade route crosses. Points for a route that traverses this many geographical regions are as follows:

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 4 | 8 | 12 | 17 | 23 | 30 |

* Points for controlling geographical regions by having the most trading centers there

  * In each region, the player with the most trading centers in that region receives: 11 pts; second place: 7 pts; third place: 4 pts. Ties share the points, rounding down.

### **Victory**

* The player with the highest score wins  
* Tie breakers are: number of trade routes, then number of trading centers, then number of rare goods
